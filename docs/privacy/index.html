Ascendio Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 21, 2026

Ascendio is an incremental game made by an independent developer. This page explains what data the game uses and why. In short: no account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking. The only thing that leaves your device is your leaderboard entry.

What stays on your device

Your game progress and settings are saved on your device only (in the browser's local storage for the web version). Reminder notifications are scheduled locally by your phone; no server sends them. Uninstalling the game (or clearing the site data on the web) deletes all of it.

The leaderboard

To rank players, the game sends this to the Ascendio leaderboard server:

  • a random player ID, generated on your device and not linked to you, your device or any account;
  • your player name, a random name until you choose one in the settings;
  • your game scores: RAD, species found and your current animal.

Your player name and scores are visible to other players on the leaderboard. Pick a name that does not identify you if you prefer to stay anonymous. This data is used only to show the leaderboard and to reject impossible scores.

The server runs on Cloudflare, which, like any web host, processes your IP address to deliver the request. The game does not store your IP address.

Update check (GitHub version only)

The Android version downloaded from GitHub checks GitHub for a newer release at launch, so GitHub receives your IP address (see GitHub's privacy statement). The Google Play version does not do this; Google Play handles updates.

Sharing

Data is never sold or shared with anyone, apart from the hosting providers named above, which process it only to run the service.

Keeping and deleting your data

A leaderboard entry is kept as long as the leaderboard exists. Resetting your game in the settings replaces your score. To have your entry deleted, email the address below with your player name.

Children

Ascendio does not knowingly collect personal information from children. The leaderboard asks for no personal information; a child can play with the random name the game gives them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
